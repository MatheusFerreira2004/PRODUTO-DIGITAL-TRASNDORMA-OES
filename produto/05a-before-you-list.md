# Before you list

For readers preparing to sell. Buyers usually see your house first in a listing photo, then from the car. Work from that view.

[IMAGE: beforelist_street_view · the same house seen from the street, framed like a listing photo]

## Look at it like a buyer
- [ ] Take a photo from the street, from where a listing photo would be taken.
- [ ] Note the first three things your eye goes to. Fix those first.
- [ ] Walk from the footpath to the door and note anything loose, broken or dirty.

## Fix what a buyer will notice
- [ ] Clutter, bins and dead plants gone (*10 quick wins*, #1)
- [ ] Lawn mowed and edged, beds mulched
- [ ] Front door clean or freshly painted; hardware working
- [ ] Broken pickets, railings, gutters or lights repaired
- [ ] Windows clean, screens in place

## Keep it neutral
- Light neutral walls with one door colour usually suit the widest range of buyers.
- Leave bold palettes for a house you plan to keep.

## Be honest
- **Do not paint over a problem.** Cracks, stains and soft wood may be a sign of moisture or movement. Get them checked; covering them can create problems later with inspections and disclosure.
- **Do not use AI "after" images as listing photos.** They show a possible result, not the house for sale.
- **Know your disclosure rules.** In the US, sellers of homes built before 1978 must disclose known lead-based paint information. In Canada and Australia, rules vary by province, state and territory: ask your agent or a local lawyer or conveyancer.

## Timing
- Allow drying time before the listing photos: paint and fresh mulch look best a few days after the work, not the same day.
- Plants settle in a week or two. Plant early if you can.
