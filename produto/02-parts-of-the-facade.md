# Parts of the facade

Every house has the same building blocks. Change them one at a time and the whole front changes. Each part shows what makes the biggest difference, who does it and the most common mistake.

---

## Front door
[IMAGE: parte_porta — the same door in five colours]

The door is the cheapest, fastest focal point. People look at it first.

- **Choose a colour that contrasts with the walls:** dark door on light walls, or a light or bright door on dark walls.
- **Match the house's mood:** deep red, navy or black for traditional houses; blue-grey, sage or teal for cottages and bungalows; natural wood, black or a bold colour for modern houses.
- **Change the hardware too:** handle, knocker, kick plate and house numbers in one finish (black, bronze or brass).
- **Paint in good weather:** dry, within the temperature range on the paint label, no rain forecast.

| Job | Who | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Clean and paint the door | DIY | Low |
| New handle in the existing holes | DIY | Low |
| Replace the whole door or frame | Pro | — |

**Most common mistake:** painting over a dirty, glossy door without sanding and priming. The paint peels around the handle.

## Windows and shutters
[IMAGE: parte_janelas — shutters added, removed and painted]

- **Clean glass and frames first.** Dirty windows make a house look empty.
- **Shutters should look like they could close:** each one about half the width of the window and the full height. Too-small shutters look fake.
- **No room for proper shutters?** Leave them off and paint the window trim instead.
- **Window boxes** suit cottages and Cape Cods; keep them the width of the window.

| Job | Who | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Clean windows and frames | DIY (ground floor) | Low |
| Paint shutters and trim | DIY, check first on old houses | Medium |
| Add or remove shutters | DIY on ground floor | Medium |
| Replace windows | Pro | — |

**Most common mistake:** work above the ground floor from a ladder. Upper-floor windows and trim are a professional job unless you have the right access equipment and experience.

## Walls

What you can do depends on the material.

### Siding / weatherboard (wood, vinyl, fibre cement)
[IMAGE: parte_paredes_siding]
- **Wash gently** with a soft brush and a cleaner for exterior walls. High-pressure washing can force water behind boards and damage old paint.
- **Wood:** scrape only loose paint, prime bare spots, then paint. Check for lead first on old houses.
- **Vinyl:** usually cleaned, not painted. Painting vinyl darker than the original can make it warp in the sun; check what the manufacturer allows.
- **Australia:** flat sheets on older houses may be asbestos cement ("fibro"). Do not sand, drill, cut or pressure-wash until tested.

### Brick
[IMAGE: parte_paredes_tijolo]
- **Clean before you decide to paint.** Brick often looks new after a gentle wash.
- **Painting brick is hard to undo.** It also needs repainting every few years. Try trim and door colours first.
- **Crumbling mortar** needs repointing; small areas can be DIY, large areas are a pro job.

### Render / stucco
[IMAGE: parte_paredes_reboco]
- **Hairline cracks** can be filled with an exterior filler and painted with masonry paint.
- **Wider cracks, bulges or hollow-sounding patches:** check first. They can mean moisture or movement.

| Job | Who | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Gentle wash | DIY | Low |
| Paint ground-floor walls | DIY, check first on old houses | High |
| Two-storey walls | Pro | — |
| Wide cracks, bulges, rot | Check first / Pro | — |

**Most common mistake:** choosing a wall colour from a small card. Paint a large sample board and look at it outside in morning and afternoon light.

## Entry and porch / verandah
[IMAGE: parte_entrada — before and after of one entry]

The entry is where people stop, so details count.

- **Symmetry:** a pair of matching pots or plants either side of the door.
- **Mailbox / letterbox and house numbers:** one finish, matching the door hardware.
- **Doormat:** larger than you think, roughly the width of the door or wider.
- **Porch ceiling and floor:** clean or repaint; they show in every photo.
- **Lighting:** new wired lights are a job for a licensed electrician. Replacing a fitting is still electrical work in many places. Solar or battery lights are a DIY alternative.

| Job | Who | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Pots, mat, mailbox, numbers | DIY | Low |
| Paint porch floor and railings | DIY, check first on old houses | Medium |
| Loose railings, soft boards, sagging porch | Pro | — |
| Wired lights | Pro (electrician) | — |

**Most common mistake:** lots of small decorations. Fewer, larger pieces look intentional.

## Garden
[IMAGE: parte_jardim — the four layouts below]

In most of the example houses, the garden does half of the transformation.

### Four layouts that suit almost any house
1. **Foundation bed:** a curved or straight bed along the front wall, 3-5 ft (0.9-1.5 m) deep, with low shrubs and mulch. Hides the base of the house.
2. **Framed entry:** one taller plant or small shrub each side of the steps.
3. **Lined path:** low plants or a neat edge along both sides of the path.
4. **Corner anchor:** one small tree or large shrub at a front corner, to soften the house edge.

**Keep plants away from the walls:** leave at least 2 ft (60 cm) between shrubs and the house, so walls can dry and you can paint.

### Plants by climate
Use these as types to ask for at your local nursery. They know what grows where you live.

| Climate | Good types | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Cold (snowy winters) | Hardy evergreen shrubs, ornamental grasses, perennials that die back in winter | Plants that need frost-free winters |
| Temperate | Low hedging shrubs, flowering shrubs, perennials | Fast-growing climbers on walls |
| Hot and dry | Drought-tolerant shrubs, succulents, grasses, gravel mulch | Thirsty lawns where water is restricted |
| Humid / subtropical | Leafy tropical-look shrubs, palms in pots, ground covers | Dense planting against walls (damp) |

| Job | Who | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Clear overgrowth, mow, edge | DIY | Medium |
| Beds, mulch, shrubs | DIY | Medium |
| Remove trees, work near power lines | Pro | — |

**Safety:** in the US and Canada, wild climbers can be poison ivy. Wear gloves and long sleeves, and never burn it. Call your local utility-locating service before digging deep holes.

**Most common mistake:** planting one of everything. Repeat three to five types of plant along the front.

## Path, driveway and fence
[IMAGE: parte_caminho]

- **Clean and edge:** a cleaned path with a crisp lawn edge looks new.
- **Weeds in cracks:** pull them and fill joints with sand or a joint filler.
- **Fences:** replace broken pickets, then paint or stain the whole fence one colour.

| Job | Who | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Clean, weed, edge | DIY | Low |
| Replace pickets, paint fence | DIY | Medium |
| New concrete path, steps or driveway | Pro | — |
| Fence posts loose or leaning | Check first / Pro | — |

**Most common mistake:** pressure-washing old paving or painted surfaces too hard. It can etch concrete, strip mortar and spray old paint chips around the yard.
